Imagine the job ends tomorrow. Your access is gone. Then someone asks what you actually accomplished there.

You remember the projects. But the dashboard is inside a system you cannot open anymore, and the decision trail lived in meetings you no longer attend.

Your experience did not disappear. Your access to the evidence did.

This is not a video about predicting a layoff, and it is not a reason to take company files. Access to a document does not mean you are entitled to keep it, and removing a company name does not make restricted material yours to retain. No forwarding internal emails, downloading dashboards, copying customer information, or collecting employee records.

It is a simple evidence habit:

Capture the contribution, not the calendar.
Qualify the claim: confirmed, qualified, or not yet verified.
Make it retrievable, named by the problem rather than the project code.

This habit does not prevent a layoff. It does not determine severance, benefits, access rights, or hiring decisions. Its job is narrower: make an unexpected change less likely to erase the details you are entitled to use.
