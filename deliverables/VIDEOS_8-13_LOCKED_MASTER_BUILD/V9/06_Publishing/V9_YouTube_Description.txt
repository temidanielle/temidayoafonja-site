You can be very experienced and still read one sentence in a job description that makes you feel like a beginner again: "Direct industry experience required."

Do not make either mistake. Do not say "I can already do all of this." And do not say "None of my experience counts here."

Separate three things instead:

Capability. What underlying work can you actually prove?
Context. What changes when the setting changes, and which of those changes is real?
Credentials. What is a confirmed requirement, what is a preference, and what have you simply assumed?

The implementation role in this video is a constructed example, not a live vacancy and not a claim that every role of that kind has the same requirements.

The same verb in two roles does not prove equivalent work. "Approve" can mean checking a routine item in one place and accepting a consequential risk in another.

A good explanation cannot erase bias, a weak market, a closed hiring rule, or a real experience gap. Its job is narrower. It stops you from describing all your experience as either fully portable or worthless.
