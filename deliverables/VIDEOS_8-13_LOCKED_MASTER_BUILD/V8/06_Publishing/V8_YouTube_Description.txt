You built it from scratch. Now everybody uses it. And that is exactly why your contribution can become harder to see.

The process already exists. It looks obvious. Maybe even easy. What disappeared from the story is everything you had to work out before it could become normal.

This video puts that back, without making the work sound dramatic:

Reconstruct the before. What could people not do consistently before your work existed?
Show the judgment. Not every action, but the decisions that made the solution usable.
Keep the proof. Separate three levels of evidence that are not interchangeable.

EXISTENCE means it was created. USE means people actually used it. EFFECT means you have evidence that something important changed. A process can exist without being used, and be used without producing the outcome you hoped for. Choose the strongest level your evidence actually supports.

By the end you will have one four-sentence account of something you built that a person who was not there can still understand.

The intake process in this video is an illustration, not a real case. The life-sciences example is a bounded public account: no manager counts, no baselines, and no claim that everyone became more capable because a framework existed.

And proof does not mean taking your employer's files. Keep your own account and information you are permitted to retain.
