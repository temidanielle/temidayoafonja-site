You have saved the jobs. Rewritten the headline. Maybe started a course. And you still do not know whether you actually want the work you are preparing for.

That is the problem with career activity. It can make you feel productive without answering the decision.

So before a high-cost commitment, choose one destination and test it for thirty days. Not to prove you can change careers in a month, but to answer four questions: what the work is really asking for, what you already have evidence for, what you would still need to learn, and whether the option fits the life you actually have.

Define. Investigate. Try. Decide.

Days 1 to 7: map what the role needs against what you can already support.
Days 8 to 14: ask people who know the work about the work, not about your potential.
Days 15 to 21: try one bounded piece using public, synthetic, or otherwise permitted material. Label it a work sample, not professional experience.
Days 22 to 30: continue, modify, or stop. And if the evidence is not enough, call the result inconclusive.

This is a plan, not a completed experiment. The schedule is adaptable, and the test only works if "not this option" is allowed to be a good result.
