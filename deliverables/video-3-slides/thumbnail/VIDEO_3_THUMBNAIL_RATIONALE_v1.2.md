# Video 3 Thumbnail — Visual Hypothesis v1.2

Video: 3 Things to Do Before Quitting Your Job
Words: WAIT BEFORE YOU QUIT (locked, not reopened)

## The hypothesis being tested

Someone who is close to resigning is not browsing. They are already leaning
toward a decision and are scanning for either permission or a reason to pause.
The thumbnail has one job: interrupt that lean for long enough that the title
can do the rest.

Three consequences follow.

**The words carry the interruption, not the image.** WAIT is an instruction, and
it is the first thing read in both layouts. It is a hard stop that costs the
viewer nothing to obey. The remaining two lines convert it from a shout into a
specific offer.

**The face supplies the reason to trust the instruction.** The expression in the
approved portrait is calm and considered rather than alarmed. That matters here.
A worried face would frame the video as a warning about risk, which is the
opposite of what the script does. A composed face frames it as a person who has
seen this decision made well and badly, which is what the early proof beat in the
script actually claims.

**Nothing else competes.** No arrows, no circles, no red, no invented statistic,
no numbered badge, no tick device. The palette is the same Capability Formation
navy, cream and gold the decks use, so the thumbnail and the first frame of the
video do not contradict each other.

## Why the number 3 is not on the thumbnail

The composition studies carried a gold "3" and three check rows. Both were
dropped.

The title already says "3 Things", and it sits directly under the thumbnail on
every surface. Repeating the number spends the strongest area of the frame on
information the viewer is about to get free. Worse, the check rows were bars with
no words in them, which at small sizes read as text that failed to load rather
than as a graphic device.

Removing both let the type grow. That is the trade that actually improves
performance at 200 px.

## Final A, recommended

Portrait right, headline left on cream, gold rule on the seam, gold underscore
closing the block.

Reasons to lead with this one:

- Her gaze travels right to left, into the words. In Final B the eyeline runs the
  same direction as the reading order, so the face and the copy compete for the
  first fixation instead of handing off to each other.
- The cream field holds a hard edge against a dark UI. In the dark mode
  simulation, Final B's navy panel softens into the surrounding page and the
  thumbnail loses roughly a third of its apparent width. Final A keeps its full
  footprint in both themes, which is the single clearest difference between them.
- Navy on cream is the highest contrast pairing available in the palette, and it
  is the pairing the decks already use for primary statements.

## Final B, alternate

Portrait left, navy panel right, WAIT carried in gold.

Worth holding because the gold WAIT on navy is the loudest single element either
layout produces, and in a light mode feed surrounded by white cards, the navy
block is the more aggressive interrupt. If the video is being placed against
competitors using bright, high-saturation thumbnails, B is the one that fights
for attention rather than standing apart from it.

Its weakness is the dark mode edge loss described above.

## What was checked

Both layouts were rendered at 640, 360 and 200 px, and simulated on the mobile
home feed, mobile search results and the desktop right column in both light and
dark mode.

At 200 px all three words remain legible in both layouts, and the face remains
identifiable in both. That was the pass condition, and both meet it. The
recommendation between them rests on eyeline and on dark mode edge retention, not
on legibility.

## Not decided here

Nothing in the script, deck, title, CTA, safety boundary or Watch Next was
touched. The thumbnail was built to the words already locked.
