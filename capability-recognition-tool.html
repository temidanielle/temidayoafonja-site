The Capability Recognition Tool

Three patterns quietly stop capability growth.

Select the one that describes your last 90 days. Not the one that flatters you — the one you recognize when you stop performing for the room.

Pattern 01 · Repeating, Not Stretching
You are doing this year what you did last year, well.

The work is competent, the feedback is positive, and the edges of what you can actually do have not moved in twelve months or more. You are performing, not building. This pattern is easy to miss because it feels like stability. It shows up when you can predict the outcome of your work before you start it — when nothing on your desk requires you to find out whether you can do something you have not already proven you can do.

Pattern 02 · Indispensable, But Mainly Here
You are essential inside these walls, and nowhere else.

Your organization would struggle to replace you. That fact has quietly become the whole plan. The specific combination of context, relationships, and institutional memory that makes you valuable does not exist as a job title anywhere else. This pattern shows up as comfort that resists examination — you have not tested what you are worth outside this building because the answer, honestly, is not obviously in your favor yet.

Pattern 03 · Responsibility > Capability
Your scope grew faster than your proof did.

Title, headcount, or mandate has expanded, and it reads as progress. But responsibility is what you were given; capability is what you have actually demonstrated under scrutiny. The gap between the two is the part nobody puts in the promotion announcement. This pattern shows up as a quiet, specific anxiety — a sense that you are one hard question away from being asked to prove something you have not yet had to prove.
