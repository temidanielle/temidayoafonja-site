Evidence

Enterprise work that shaped the Capability Formation Framework.

Before founding The Density Group, I spent nearly two decades working inside organizations where capability had to survive growth, regulation, acquisition, leadership transition, and changes in the work itself.

A note on the work

These examples are drawn from enterprise operating roles held before Temidayo Afonja founded The Density Group. They show the organizational conditions from which the Capability Formation framework was developed. Organization names and identifying details are generalized where necessary.

Selected Founder Operating Evidence

What changed in the system.

Each example is organized around the business condition, the work I led, the documented outcome, and the Capability Formation insight that followed.

01
Global B2B SaaS

Rebuilding onboarding as a capability environment.

The organization needed more than a better welcome. It needed new employees to understand the work, form the right relationships, and become effective without losing the judgment the business depended on.

Source of work: Senior Director, Employee Experience & Belonging

The operating challenge

Growth and post-acquisition change had made the early employee experience uneven. Listening data showed that employees could complete onboarding activity without becoming fully integrated into the organization or clear about how to contribute.

What I led

An end-to-end redesign of onboarding and early integration, including employee journey architecture, listening analysis, manager accountability, communication, and the infrastructure required to make the experience measurable rather than ceremonial.

Documented outcomes

47 to 75Movement in the onboarding integration driver
+46Point increase in onboarding NPS
30%Retention improvement within 18 months
$2M+Estimated turnover cost avoidance

What it revealed

Onboarding is an early capability formation environment. The organization is already teaching people what judgment matters, whose knowledge counts, and how quickly they can become useful beyond the tasks on a checklist.

02
Global Life Sciences

Building leadership capability at global scale.

A regulated global organization needed a common way to develop managers without pretending that leadership looked identical across functions, regions, and operating conditions.

Source of work: Enterprise leadership and advisory role held before founding The Density Group

The operating challenge

Leadership expectations existed across the organization, but the system needed a clearer connection between what leaders were expected to know, the decisions they were expected to make, and the environments in which those capabilities would actually be tested.

What I led

An enterprise leadership capability framework and manager enablement approach designed for use across a global, regulated environment. The work translated broad leadership expectations into experiences, practices, and support that managers could use in real operating conditions.

Documented outcome

1,000+Managers reached globally through enterprise leadership capability work

What it revealed

A capability framework becomes real only when it changes the work leaders are trusted to carry. Competencies describe. Experience, feedback, and consequential decisions form.

03
Enterprise Technology

Protecting capability through acquisition and transition.

A successful integration had to preserve more than roles and reporting lines. It had to retain the people, relationships, and organizational context required for the combined business to keep operating.

Source of work: Enterprise leadership and advisory role held before founding The Density Group

The operating challenge

Acquisition created uncertainty at the same moment the organization most needed continuity. Critical talent risk, fragmented communication, and unclear ownership could have turned a structural transition into a capability loss event.

What I led

The workforce integration architecture for the first 90 days, including transition communication, listening, manager support, employee journey design, and focused attention to the people and knowledge the organization could not afford to lose.

Documented outcomes

90 daysInitial post-acquisition workforce integration designed and delivered
0Critical-role departures recorded during the 90-day integration period

What it revealed

Retention is not only a headcount outcome. During transition, organizations must preserve the context, relationships, and judgment that allow important work to continue after the structure changes.

What the work established

The framework began as an operating observation.

Across industries, the same pattern kept appearing: organizations measured activity, performance, and retention more carefully than they measured what the work was building.

01

Capability is formed through consequential work.

Courses and competency models matter, but people become more capable through the problems, decisions, feedback, and exposure their roles repeatedly provide.

02

Internal value and portable capability are not the same.

An employee can become indispensable inside one system while becoming less able to move across functions, environments, or future priorities.

03

Resilience depends on what survives transition.

Organizations remain capable when judgment, relationships, and institutional knowledge can be transferred rather than disappearing with a person or structure.
