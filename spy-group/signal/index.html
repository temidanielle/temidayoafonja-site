Paths to award

Four ways to put
work here, by ceiling.

SDVOSB status is not a courtesy line on a website. It is an acquisition authority. These are the mechanisms a buyer can actually use, from a card purchase up to a sole-source award.

$10KCeiling

Purchase card

Micro-purchase. A card holder buys direct, no competition required, and the spend counts toward the agency's small-business and veteran goals.

$250KCeiling

Simplified acquisition

Streamlined procedures below the simplified acquisition threshold. This range is reserved for small business. Quotes turned in a week, not a quarter.

$4.5MServices & supplies

Set-aside or sole source

A contracting officer may set an acquisition aside for service-disabled veteran-owned small businesses, or award sole-source when the criteria are met. Manufacturing carries a higher ceiling.

NoneSubcontract

Teaming

For primes carrying a small-business subcontracting plan, SDVOSB subcontract dollars count against the plan's goals. Joint ventures and mentor-protégé welcome.

Thresholds reflect current FAR small-business rules and are reviewed periodically. Confirm the figure that applies to your acquisition before relying on it.

Differentiators

Why the file
closes cleanly.

Three reasons a buyer or a prime ends up better off, written the way they would have to justify the choice in writing.

Award authority,
not just a label

Service-disabled veteran-owned status opens set-aside and sole-source paths that shorten a procurement timeline and credit against agency goaling. The categories stack: veteran, minority-owned, small.

Documentation
as a habit

Founded and led by a veteran. Custody, receipt, and price reasonableness are documented as the work happens, so the contract file is defensible on the day it is audited rather than reconstructed later.

Sized for
the work

Small enough that a purchase-card order gets the same attention as a task order, and direct enough that the person who answers the phone is the person accountable for the delivery date.
