Choose one accomplishment worth remembering.

Do not try to reconstruct your entire career. Choose one project, assignment, decision, problem, launch, redesign, client engagement, process improvement or piece of work from the recent past that mattered. Then complete the six prompts that follow.

Choose something where at least one is true

  • Something changed because you were involved.
  • You exercised judgment, not just activity.
  • You solved or prevented a meaningful problem.
  • Someone relied on your expertise.
  • You learned something you could use again.

Quick capture

Write it down fast, before the detail fades. Two lines is enough.

Before you write

Tick each one before you write. If you cannot tick it, leave that detail out.

  • I am recording my own recollection, and information I am permitted to retain.
  • I am not copying, forwarding, photographing or reproducing confidential or employer-owned material.
  • I am not including protected customer, employee or proprietary company information.
  • Where I am unsure whether I may retain something, I have left it out.

This is educational material, not legal advice.

What actually happened?

You do not need a perfect metric. You need a truthful account of what changed.

What evidence supports this?

What makes the account defensible? Use only accurate and permitted evidence: a measured result, an observable change, a public source, documented feedback, a role-based verifier, or a before and after condition.

Your portable proof line

Turn the evidence into one portable line.

I helped [solve / change / build / improve what]
by [your specific contribution or judgment],
which [result or observable change],
showing that I can [portable capability].

This is a scaffold for thinking, not a sentence to copy mechanically. Rearrange it so it sounds like you.

Before

“Led Project Horizon onboarding work.”

Portable

“Led a cross-functional onboarding redesign that improved new-hire integration by diagnosing gaps, aligning stakeholders and turning an unclear experience problem into an operating change.”

My portable proof line

Can it travel?

  • Someone outside my company could understand it.
  • It separates my contribution from the team result.
  • I could defend it if someone asked a second question.
  • I have removed anything I am not permitted to retain or disclose.
