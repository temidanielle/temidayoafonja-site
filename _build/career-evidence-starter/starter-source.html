Before you start

Choose one accomplishment worth remembering.

Do not try to reconstruct your entire career.

Choose one project, assignment, decision, problem, launch, redesign, client engagement, process improvement or piece of work from the recent past that mattered. Then complete the six prompts that follow.

Choose something where at least one is true

  • Something changed because you were involved.
  • You exercised judgment, not just activity.
  • You solved or prevented a meaningful problem.
  • Someone relied on your expertise.
  • You learned something you could use again.

Quick capture

Write it down fast, before the detail fades. Two lines is enough.

Why it mattered

Permission checklist

Tick each one before you write. If you cannot tick it, leave that detail out.

  • I am recording my own recollection, and information I am permitted to retain.
  • I am not copying, forwarding, photographing or reproducing confidential or employer-owned material.
  • I am not including protected customer, employee or proprietary company information.
  • Where I am unsure whether I may retain something, I have left it out.

This is educational material, not legal advice.

Build the evidence

What actually happened?

Project or work event

What piece of work are you capturing?

1What was true before I got involved?

Describe the starting condition, problem, constraint or need.

2What was my specific contribution or judgment?

Your part, not everything the team did. What did you notice, decide, influence, build, change or prevent?

3What changed?

What improved, became possible, moved, disappeared or was prevented? If there is no number, describe the change in words.

4What evidence supports this?

Use only accurate and permitted evidence: a measured result, an observable change, a public source, documented feedback, a role-based verifier, or a before and after condition.

Find what travels

What does this work show you can do?

5What could I plausibly do again?

Move beyond the project name. What did this work require you to know how to do? It might be to diagnose an unclear problem, bring structure to ambiguity, influence across functions, improve a process, make a difficult tradeoff, translate technical information, build trust, lead through change, identify risk or redesign an experience.

6Remove the internal language

Which words only make sense inside your current company?

Internal project name

Internal acronym or system

Company-specific language

How would you explain the same work to someone who has never worked there?

Your portable proof line

Turn the evidence into one portable line.

I helped [solve / change / build / improve what]
by [your specific contribution or judgment],
which [result or observable change],
showing that I can [portable capability].

This is a scaffold for thinking, not a sentence to copy mechanically. Rearrange it so it sounds like you.

Before

“Led Project Horizon onboarding work.”

Portable

“Led a cross-functional onboarding redesign that improved how quickly new employees became integrated, requiring me to diagnose gaps across teams, coordinate stakeholders and turn an unclear experience problem into a measurable operating change.”

My portable proof line

Before you use it

  • Someone outside my company could understand it.
  • It separates my contribution from the team result.
  • I could defend it if someone asked a second question.
  • I have removed anything I am not permitted to retain or disclose.
