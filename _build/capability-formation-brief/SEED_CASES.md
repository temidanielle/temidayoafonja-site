# Seed Cases

The Capability Formation Brief: two worked case notes.

Version 1.0 draft for review. Not final until approved.

These are written to the casework template. Each uses the headings that carry it, not all ten. They are anonymized and illustrative. They demonstrate the read, they do not describe a specific real person.

---

## Case 1

Library category: Career Portability

### CASE
"I have been here seventeen years. Another company wants direct industry experience, and I do not have it in their industry."

### WHAT THE READER IS AFRAID MAY BE TRUE
That seventeen years in one industry has produced experience that only counts in that industry, and that the wall the other company put up, direct industry experience, is a polite way of saying the years do not transfer.

### WHAT I WOULD NOT ASSUME
I would not assume that "direct industry experience" means what it literally says. It is often a proxy the hiring side reaches for because it is easy to screen on, standing in for something harder to name: will this person understand our constraints fast enough, and will they need a long runway we cannot afford. Those are answerable without having worked in the industry. I also would not assume seventeen years is one kind of experience. Seventeen years usually contains several different capabilities stacked in one place, and the reader has probably stopped seeing the range because it all wears the same employer's name.

### WHAT TRAVELS
The judgment built by seniority travels almost entirely. How to operate when the information is incomplete, how to read an organization, how to carry a decision that others will second guess, how to bring a function through a change. None of that is industry bound. The specific problems the reader has solved travel as problem shapes even when the industry does not match, because most senior work is the same small set of problems wearing different clothes.

### WHAT DOES NOT
The regulatory detail, the named systems, the relationships, and the industry specific vocabulary do not travel. Neither does the assumption, if the reader holds it, that everyone will already know the context they operate in. In the new industry they will not, and leading with local vocabulary reads as narrowness rather than depth.

### WHAT CAN BE PROVED
The transferable judgment is provable if the reader has kept, or can reconstruct, accounts of specific decisions and what changed because of them. What is harder to prove on paper is speed of adaptation, which is exactly the thing the industry-experience screen is trying to protect against. That gap is the real work.

### WHAT THE OTHER SIDE MAY BE TRYING TO DE-RISK
Ramp time and fit. The industry-experience requirement is the hiring side trying not to bet on a long, expensive learning curve. If the reader can show they have entered an unfamiliar domain before and become useful quickly, they are answering the actual worry rather than the literal requirement.

### THE BOUNDARY
The reader cannot manufacture industry experience they do not have, and should not pretend to. The honest move is not to argue the requirement away. It is to make the transferable judgment legible and to produce evidence of fast adaptation, so the requirement stops being the only lens.

### WHAT I WOULD TEST NEXT
One test. Take the two strongest pieces of the seventeen years, strip every industry-specific word, and rewrite them as problem, judgment, and outcome that a person in the target industry would recognize. If those two translate into something the other side would find credible, the barrier is legibility, and it is workable. If they collapse without the industry context, the barrier is more real, and the reader has learned something true before spending months on applications. Either result is useful. The tools that fit next are What Travels / What Stays Here and The Long-Tenure Translation Test.

## Case 2

Library category: Readiness

### CASE
"My manager says I need more exposure."

### WHAT THE READER IS AFRAID MAY BE TRUE
That "more exposure" is a soft no. That the real message is "not you, not now," and that exposure is a moving target that will never be declared met.

### WHAT I WOULD NOT ASSUME
I would not assume "exposure" means one thing, and I would not assume it is either a genuine development note or a brush off. It is usually vague because the manager has a real sense of a gap and has not done the work to name it. The reader's job is not to accept the word or to resent it. It is to convert it into something specific enough to act on or to test. I read "exposure" through the approved 4R, because the word almost always hides one of the four.

### THE 4R READ
Responsibility: has the reader actually owned outcomes at the next level's scope, or owned tasks while someone else carried the outcome? If the gap is here, "exposure" means "I have not seen you hold the bag."

Range: has the reader operated across enough different situations, functions, or stakeholders, or deep in one lane? If the gap is here, "exposure" means "I have only seen you in one setting."

Recognition: do the people who decide actually know what the reader has done? If the gap is here, "exposure" is not a capability gap at all. It is a visibility gap, and the reader is more ready than the room knows.

Recovery: has the reader been seen handling something going wrong, not just things going well? If the gap is here, "exposure" means "I have not seen you under load."

Most "needs more exposure" notes resolve to Range or Recognition. Those are very different problems. Range is real work to close. Recognition is closable in weeks by making existing work visible to the right people, and mistaking one for the other wastes months.

### WHAT EVIDENCE WOULD CHANGE THE READ
A single specific from the manager. The most useful next move is one question back to them: "When you say exposure, which of these would move it most: owning a bigger outcome, working across more of the business, or the leadership team seeing work I have already done?" The answer tells the reader whether they are short on a capability or short on visibility. If the manager cannot answer it, that is also information, and the reader has surfaced that the note was not yet real.

### WHAT I WOULD TEST NEXT
Two moves. First, run the 4R read to form a hypothesis about which R the manager means. Second, ask the manager the one clarifying question above and compare their answer to the hypothesis. Where they agree, the reader has a named, specific gap and can build a plan against that one R. Where they diverge, the reader has learned that the manager's read and their own differ, which is a more important conversation than exposure. The tool that fits next is The 4R Readiness Check, and if the answer points at visibility, the Employer Legibility Test.
