# Paid System Architecture

The Capability Formation Brief: paid application layer

Version 1.0 draft for review. Not final until approved.

---

## 1. What Capability Formation is, and where paid sits inside it

Capability Formation is the body of work. Its public front door is career portability. The public promise is plain: "I help experienced professionals make career pivots and internal moves without starting over."

The recurring question the audience carries is "What travels when the context changes?" Underneath it sits a quieter, more personal question: "Does what I have already done still count?" The honest answer is "Some of it does. Let us get precise about what."

That word, precise, is the whole reason the paid layer exists. The free work can help a reader see the question and name their situation. It cannot get them to precision about their own case. Precision requires them to test their own evidence, find their own gap, and reach their own decision. That is work the reader has to do on their own material, and it is the work the paid layer is built to carry.

The standing professional audit that runs underneath every tool:

1. What travels?
2. What does not?
3. What can I prove?
4. What must I relearn?

Every paid tool, every case note, and every clinic is a way of moving a reader through some part of that audit on their own situation.

---

## 2. The paid problem

The reader arrives at the paid layer already convinced by the free work. They understand the idea. They can repeat it back. What they cannot do is answer it for themselves.

The paid problem, stated as the reader would state it:

"I understand the idea, but I still do not know what it means for my situation."

They do not need more explanation. They need a way to apply the thinking to their own years of work, test what they actually have, locate the real gap rather than the feared one, and decide what to do next. General education cannot do this, because the reader is not short on understanding. They are short on a clear read of their own case.

---

## 3. The transformation

Before:

"I understand the idea, but I still do not know what it means for my situation."

After:

"I can identify what travels, what does not, what I can prove, what I need to relearn, and what I should test or decide next."

The paid layer is judged on that shift and nothing else. If a reader finishes a tool and still cannot say what travels, what they can prove, and what they should test next, the tool has failed, no matter how well it reads.

---

## 4. Free is SEE and NAME. Paid is TEST and DECIDE.

This is the line that keeps the two layers from collapsing into each other.

Free = SEE + NAME.
The free work helps the reader recognize the problem and give it a name. It builds recognition. A reader leaves free content able to see a pattern in their situation that they could not see before, and able to name it. Recognition is the job. It is enough on its own to be worth reading, and it is deliberately not enough to reach a decision.

Paid = TEST + DECIDE.
The paid work helps the reader apply the thinking to their own situation, test their own evidence against it, find the actual gap rather than the assumed one, and make a better decision. Paid does not re-explain the idea. It hands the reader instruments and puts their own case on the table.

Practical consequence: if a draft paid tool spends most of its space teaching the concept again, it has drifted back into SEE and NAME and needs to be redesigned. A paid tool should assume the reader already sees the problem and move straight to testing their case.

---

## 5. What the paid layer is not

The paid model has to scale. That single constraint rules out the shapes this offer must never take:

- Not more essays.
- Not more general education.
- Not unlimited question and answer.
- Not one to one coaching.
- Not resume reviews.
- Not personalized document reviews.
- Not unlimited direct messages.
- Not guaranteed individual feedback.
- Not a daily community obligation.

Every one of those trades the operator's time linearly for the reader's outcome. The paid layer instead ships instruments the reader runs on their own material, plus casework that shows the reasoning once and is read many times. The operator's effort is spent making, not answering.

---

## 6. How paid differs from the standalone products

There are already two paid standalone products in the family, and the paid layer must not blur into either:

- Keep the Proof is a one time purchase. It is the complete system for capturing, translating, organizing, and maintaining career evidence over time. It is a system the reader owns and reuses.
- The Field Kit is a one time purchase. It reads what the current position is building, whether it travels, and what direction may be worth testing.

The paid layer of the Brief is different in kind. It is a subscription to an accumulating library of decision equipment and casework, refreshed on a fixed cadence, organized by reader problem. Where the standalone products are systems a reader completes, the paid layer is a shelf the reader returns to whenever a new decision lands. A reader can hold all three without redundancy: the Brief tools help them decide, Keep the Proof helps them record and maintain, the Field Kit reads the current seat.

The commercial ladder, stated once so it stays clean:

- Free: Career Evidence Starter and the free Brief. Turn one project into portable proof, and see and name the problem.
- Paid one time: Keep the Proof. Build and maintain the complete career evidence system.
- Paid one time: Capability Formation Field Kit. Read what the current position is building, whether it travels, and what to test.
- Paid subscription: the Capability Formation Brief paid layer. Test and decide, month over month, on the reader's own situation, with an accumulating library and quarterly clinics.

## 7. Sustainable publishing cadence

The cadence is fixed and small, so it can be held indefinitely without the offer degrading into unpaid consulting.

Each month:
- One practical decision tool.
- One case based working note.

Each quarter:
- One live Capability Formation Case Clinic, working two to three selected anonymized submitted situations. The replay enters the paid archive.

That is the entire recurring commitment. Twelve tools and twelve case notes a year, plus four clinics and their replays, become an archive that is worth the subscription on its back catalogue alone within the first year. The clinic is the only live element, and it is capped at two to three cases so it never becomes open ended.

---

## 8. Recommended library organization

The library is organized by reader problem, not by publication date. A subscriber does not arrive asking "what shipped in March." They arrive with a live decision and need to find the instrument that fits it. The five categories map to the situations the audience actually shows up in:

- READINESS: the reader has been told, or suspects, they are not ready for a move.
- CAREER PORTABILITY: the reader needs to know what carries when the context changes.
- ROLE DECISIONS: the reader is weighing whether the current role still builds their future.
- EMPLOYER LEGIBILITY: the reader needs to know what the other side would still have to believe.
- WORK MOVEMENT: the reader's role or work is being reorganized around them.

Where each tool bank item lands is set out in PAID_TOOL_BANK.md. New tools are filed into these categories on release, so the library stays navigable by problem no matter how large it grows. Case notes are cross filed by the same categories, so a reader working a readiness decision can find both the readiness tools and the readiness cases in one place.

## 9. Rules that keep the offer scalable

These are guardrails, not preferences. Breaking one is how a scalable subscription quietly turns into unpaid one to one work.

1. Ship instruments, not answers. The unit of value is a tool or a case note the reader applies themselves, never a personalized reply.
2. Hold the cadence. One tool and one case note a month, one clinic a quarter. Do not add live obligations between clinics.
3. Cap the live surface. The clinic works two to three cases, chosen by the operator, announced in advance. No open queue.
4. Casework shows reasoning, made once, read many times. A case note is written to be read by everyone, not to advise one person.
5. No guaranteed individual feedback, ever. Submissions may feed the clinic or a future case note. They do not create a promise of a reply.
6. Reuse approved language before inventing new. Lean on 3P, 4R, and MOVE where they fit. Do not manufacture a new framework per tool.
7. Every tool ends in an output. A diagnosis, a sorted list, a named gap, a next test, a decision condition, a question to ask a manager, something to prove, or something to relearn. Never only reflection prompts.
8. Do not promise outcomes. The tools sharpen decisions. They do not guarantee a promotion, an offer, or a move.
9. Keep tools short. One to two pages, ten to twenty minutes, printable. Length is not value. A tool that needs more than two pages is usually two tools.
10. The scalability test, applied to any new idea before it ships: if delivering it well at a thousand subscribers would require the operator to read a thousand individual situations, it is the wrong shape. Redesign it as an instrument.

---

## 10. The single QA question

Before anything in the paid layer ships, it is held against one question:

"Does this help the reader make a clearer decision, or did we merely give them more career content?"

If the honest answer is "more content," it is redesigned or it does not ship. Clarity and usefulness outrank framework proliferation every time.
