# Paid Tool Bank

The Capability Formation Brief: decision tools, organized by reader problem.

Version 1.0 draft for review. Not final until approved.

This is the initial bank of ten tools. Each is one to two pages, ten to twenty minutes, printable, and ends in a usable output rather than reflection prompts. Only three carry a named structure: 3P, 4R, and MOVE. The rest are plain practical reads and checks, by design. We do not invent a new framework per tool.

A note on named language: 3P is Practice, Proof, Portability. 4R is Responsibility, Range, Recognition, Recovery. MOVE is the approved decision check for situations where a role or work is being reorganized; its canonical step names should be pulled from the approved MOVE source before that tool is built, and are not reinvented here.

Pilot build status: Tool 1 and Tool 3 are the two pilots being built first.

## READINESS

The reader has been told, or suspects, that they are not ready.

### 1. Evidence Gap or Capability Gap? (pilot)
Reader problem: "I was told I am not ready, or that I need more exposure, and I do not know whether that is true."
What it does: separates four possibilities. A, the next role needs a capability the reader genuinely has not built enough yet. B, the capability may be there but recent work has not left enough inspectable evidence. C, the reader was not given a credible chance to produce the evidence. D, more than one is true. It does not automatically side with the reader. A genuine capability gap stays a legitimate, nameable result.
Output: a named read (A, B, C, or D) and four lists. What I need to practice. What I need to prove. What access I may need. What I should test next.

### 2. The 4R Readiness Check
Reader problem: "Am I ready for the next level, and if not, on which dimension am I short?"
What it does: reads readiness across the approved 4R dimensions, Responsibility, Range, Recognition, and Recovery. The reader rates current standing and evidence on each, and finds the dimension that is actually holding the move, which is often not the one they assumed.
Output: the limiting R named, with the specific evidence or exposure that dimension is missing, and one action per short dimension.

### 10. The Relearning Boundary
Reader problem: "What can I carry into the next context, and what do I genuinely have to earn again?"
What it does: draws the line between transferable capability and capability that a new context will not accept on trust. It resolves the fourth standing-audit question, what must I relearn, into a concrete boundary rather than a worry.
Output: two columns, Carry and Earn Again, and for each item in Earn Again, the smallest credible way to start earning it.

## CAREER PORTABILITY

The reader needs to know what carries when the context changes.

### 4. What Travels / What Stays Here
Reader problem: "How much of what I have built is actually mine to take?"
What it does: a sorting sheet. The reader lists real pieces of their current work and sorts each into Travels, Stays Here, or Travels Only With Translation. It makes the standing audit's first two questions, what travels and what does not, concrete against the reader's own material.
Output: a sorted three-column inventory, with the Travels Only With Translation items flagged as the reader's translation backlog.

### 5. The Long-Tenure Translation Test
Reader problem: "I have been in one place a long time, and I am not sure my experience reads outside it."
What it does: takes the reader's strongest long-tenure work and tests it for context lock, the internal names, systems, and shorthand that only make sense inside the current employer. It surfaces where deep experience is trapped in local language.
Output: a translation list, each locked item paired with a portable, context-free version, plus the one piece of tenure that is strongest once translated.

## ROLE DECISIONS

The reader is weighing whether the current role still builds their future.

### 3. The 3P Role Read (pilot)
Reader problem: "Is my current role still building my future, or just filling my time?"
What it does: reads the current role across the approved 3P, Practice, what am I becoming better able to handle; Proof, what evidence is this work leaving behind; Portability, what could another team, function, employer, or industry plausibly use. A weak read is not converted into an automatic recommendation to resign.
Output: a three-line read, a named current trade, one capability to strengthen, and one thirty to ninety day test to run inside the current role.

### 6. The Internal Move Read
Reader problem: "Should I try to move to a different team or function inside my own company?"
What it does: reads an internal move as a portability question with a home advantage, what carries across the internal boundary, what has to be re-earned even without changing employer, and who on the other side would need to be convinced.
Output: a go, wait, or prepare read, with the one internal relationship and the one piece of evidence that would most change it.

### 7. The Stay / Leave Evidence Read
Reader problem: "Should I stay or leave, and am I deciding on evidence or on mood?"
What it does: forces a stay or leave lean to rest on inspectable evidence rather than frustration. It separates what the reader can prove about the current role's trajectory from what they merely feel about it.
Output: a lean, stay or leave, with the two or three evidence items it rests on named, and the single fact that, if it changed, would flip the lean.

## EMPLOYER LEGIBILITY

The reader needs to know what the other side would still have to believe.

### 9. The Employer Legibility Test
Reader problem: "I know what I can do. Would the person deciding on me be able to see it?"
Core question: "What would the other side still need to believe?"
What it does: reads the reader's case from the deciding side. For a target move, it names the beliefs a manager, hiring panel, or talent system would have to hold to say yes, then checks which of those beliefs the reader has made legible and which are still invisible.
Output: a belief list for the target decision, each marked visible or invisible, and for each invisible belief, the evidence that would make it legible.

## WORK MOVEMENT

The reader's role or work is being reorganized around them.

### 8. The MOVE Decision Check
Reader problem: "My role or my work is being reorganized, and I have to decide how to respond."
What it does: applies the approved MOVE decision check to a reorganization, restructure, or role change the reader did not choose. It steps the reader from the change itself to a decision about how to position, using the approved MOVE steps (canonical step names to be pulled from approved source at build time).
Output: a positioning decision for the reorganization, the one thing to protect, and the one thing to put forward, with the next conversation to have named.

## What every tool must leave behind

No tool ships as reflection prompts alone. Each leaves at least one of: a diagnosis, a sorted list, a named evidence gap, a next test, a decision condition, a question to ask a manager, something to prove, or something to relearn. If a draft leaves only questions, it is redesigned before release.
