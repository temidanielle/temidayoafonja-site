# Keep the Proof — Originality Reconciliation

Version 1.0.0 · Revised Monday, August 17, 2026 at 1:45 PM CT (America/Chicago)
Author: Temidayo Afonja, Founder and Principal, The Density Group

This document records the originality discipline applied while writing *Keep the Proof*, the specific things held clear of a referenced competitor work (Jalonni's Career Playbook), and the results of an automated phrase-overlap scan of the shipping handbook.

---

## 1. What was borrowable, and what was not

Only **high-level product-design lessons** were treated as borrowable: that a career document benefits from a clear category, from completed examples rather than empty templates, from fillable tools, and from a calm, premium visual system. These are genre conventions, not authored expression.

Nothing specific to the referenced work was reused. The following were placed on an explicit avoid-list and kept clear:

| Category | Held clear of |
|---|---|
| Title / subtitle | The competitor's title and subtitle wording |
| Section names | Its distinctive section names and running headers |
| Signature phrases | "Responsibilities vs. Results," "Task vs. Ownership," and similar coined contrasts |
| Examples | Its personas and example framings (recruiter/sales-led) |
| Visual system | Its wine/mauve + pastel palette, circular graphics, and quadrant/loop motifs |
| Layout | Its page layouts and card treatments |

## 2. How Keep the Proof differs by construction

- **Category**: "career evidence," a private permitted record — not a resume/job-search playbook.
- **Contrast pair**: "formal responsibility vs. actual ownership," authored here, distinct from the referenced work's coined pairs.
- **Workflow**: Capture / Clarify / Translate / Protect / Retrieve — an original five-step spine, presented as a strip, never an acronym.
- **Personas**: six original composites spanning operations, HR, security, engineering management, audit, and clinical research — chosen to stress confidentiality and judgment, not selling.
- **Palette**: deep navy, warm cream, muted gold, restrained rust — established independently of the referenced work; no circles or quadrants; the recurring device is a three-line "ledger" mark with a rust tick.
- **Confidentiality standard**: a first-class, load-bearing part of the product, which the referenced work does not center.

## 3. Deliberate divergence from the source memo

An earlier internal memo (Gemini) proposed a $39 price, a "defer" recommendation, specific market/Gumroad claims, and — critically — a "personal-device / off-hours loophole" suggesting that using a personal device or non-working hours could make employer information retainable.

That loophole is **unsafe and is excluded**. *Keep the Proof* teaches the opposite: ownership does not transfer with the device or the hour. The memo's price, defer recommendation, and market/Gumroad claims are also not carried into the product.

## 4. Phrase-overlap scan (automated, shipping handbook)

The handbook text was scanned for the referenced work's distinctive phrases and for prohibited frameworks. Results:

- "Responsibilities vs. Results" / "Task vs. Ownership" / "Career Playbook": **0**
- Density score / Optionality / Four Career States / Compounding-Stagnant-Fragile-Depth Trap / career state: **0**
- Resume-writing system / interview coaching / networking script / salary negotiation / job search: **0**
- "brag document": **1** (used once, to name and then move past the informal term — within the allowance)
- "stay or leave": **1** (appears only in the "what this guide deliberately does not answer" list — an exclusion, not advice)

No rewrite for overlap was required beyond the single intentional, allowed use of "brag document."

## 5. Statement

The manuscript, structure, examples, phrasing, and visual system of *Keep the Proof* are original to this product. No distinctive expression from the referenced competitor work was copied, and the unsafe guidance from the internal memo was deliberately excluded.
