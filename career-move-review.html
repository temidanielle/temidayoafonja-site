Does what you have built fit the move you are considering?

You have years of work behind you and a decision in front of you. A different function, a different industry, a step up somewhere that runs nothing like the place you are now.

The question is rarely whether you are capable. It is which parts of what you have built actually travel with you, and which parts were holding you up only because of where you were standing.

Who this is for

Experienced professionals with a specific move in mind.

This is built for people who have been working long enough to have real range, generally ten years or more, and who are now looking at a change of context. A pivot into a different field. An internal move into a function you have supported but never run. A step into an industry where none of the names on your resume mean anything.

It works best when you have something particular in view. Not "I think I want a change," but a role, a team, a direction you are seriously weighing. The review reads your evidence against that specific move, so the more specific the move, the more useful the reading.

You do not need to have bought anything first. Keep the Proof and the Field Kit are useful preparation and plenty of people find them helpful, but neither is a prerequisite and neither is assumed.

What the review works through

Four questions, asked against one real move.

Most career advice treats experience as a single quantity that either is or is not enough. It is not one quantity. Some of what you have built moves with you intact, some of it only worked because of the particular place it was built, and the difference matters enormously when the context changes.

What travels?

The judgment, the patterns and the range that hold their value in a different setting, with different people and different constraints.

What does not?

The parts that depended on relationships, institutional knowledge or authority that stays behind when you leave the building.

What can you prove?

Where the evidence is solid enough to stand up in a conversation with someone who has no reason to take your word for it.

What must you relearn?

What the new context will ask for that your current one never did, and how much of that is a real gap rather than an unfamiliar vocabulary.
