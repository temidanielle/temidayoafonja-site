Why bring this to me

I spent eighteen years on the inside. Big Four consulting at PwC, Deloitte and EY. A global life sciences organization operating under regulatory oversight. A high-growth enterprise technology company. In each of them I designed the capability system and then had to defend it to people with budget authority.

That matters for one reason. I have been in the room where these decisions get made about other people, and I have been the person the decision was made about. Most of the people advising you on workforce capability have only ever occupied one of those two positions.

What you are actually choosing between

A Big Four bench

You will get a methodology, a team that rotates, and a well-built deck. The work is real and the people are good. I trained there. What you will not get is an instrument you still own when the engagement closes. The capability to take the reading leaves with the team, and the next reading starts from zero.

Your own internal team

Your people understand the organization better than any outsider will. What they cannot do is grade it. A team scoring its own capability returns the answer the organization already believes, because everyone answering knows who reads the results. The distance is the product.

This

Four to six weeks. You keep the instrument.

What keeping the instrument means

The Organizational Instrument does not change between readings — only the answers do. Seventeen questions, fifteen scored across three equally weighted dimensions, five items each. Two dimensions set your quadrant; the third is read alongside it. Each scored answer carries a direction, and the cases that fall near a boundary are named as such by a stated rule, not a judgment call.

That reads like detail. It is the whole difference. Because the version is frozen, a reading taken in March and a reading taken in September are comparable. You can run it on one function, then on another, and set the two numbers beside each other. You can hand it to a leader who was nowhere near the original engagement and they will score it the way I would.

Most capability work produces a narrative. This produces a number you can take again.

The lens

My graduate training is in cybersecurity risk and strategy, a Master of Science from New York University, and it is the reason this work looks the way it does. That field treats a system as a risk position to be read honestly before the incident rather than explained after it. I read workforce capability the same way.

So the question I arrive with is about exposure. Where your capability is exposed, how quickly that exposure is moving, and what one change to the environment would do to it.

On AI, specifically

I have deployed AI into a live workforce at a global B2B SaaS company, and that deployment won its AI Innovation Award. I am not forecasting this shift from outside it. I have run the role redesign, the change management, and the part nobody writes about, which is what you say to a person whose job just changed underneath them.

What this is not

I do not run engagement surveys. I do not build competency libraries. I do not deliver a transformation programme. Other people do those things well and I will say so if that is what you need.
